# Answering from scoped documentation

## 1 · Find the shape of the question

Decide what kind of answer would satisfy it before searching:

- **procedural** — "how do I…" wants ordered steps and the file, command or
  endpoint they act on
- **conceptual** — "what is…", "why…" wants the model of how it works
- **reference** — "what does X accept" wants exact parameters or fields
- **local practice** — "how do *we*…" wants a procedure specific to the asker's
  organization rather than a general one

The shape decides which corpus is likely to hold it, and how much to read.

## 2 · Search, then read

Search your highest-listed granted corpus first. Search results are titles and
paths; they are not the answer. Fetch the page before making a claim from it.

If a search returns nothing in a corpus, that corpus does not cover the
question. Do not substitute another corpus's answer as though it were the same
thing, and do not fill the gap from memory. Try different terms once — the
corpora are small and keyword-matched, so a synonym is worth one retry — then
say plainly what is not covered.

## 3 · Answer

- Lead with the answer, then support it.
- Cite the path and the corpus for each claim.
- Quote exact names — a flag, a field, a path, a ticket prefix — verbatim from
  the page. Paraphrasing an identifier makes an answer unusable.
- When a more specific corpus and a more general one disagree, the more
  specific one wins, and say which you used.
- If your corpora cover only part of the question, answer the covered part and
  name the gap.

## 4 · When the corpora do not cover it

Refuse per your instructions: say you lack access to that documentation, name
what you do have, and stop. Do not approximate.
